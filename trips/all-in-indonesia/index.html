Festivals and closures to plan around

  • Christmas + New Year peak Sun 20 Dec to Tue 5 Jan

    The busiest and dearest weeks of the year across the region. Popular hostels, island boats and flights sell out. Book early and avoid one-night stops. It is worth paying for a bed you have held.

  • Ramadan + Eid (Lebaran) 8 Feb – 9/10 Mar, Mon 8 Feb to Fri 12 Mar

    A Muslim-majority part of Indonesia. Some daytime places open late or close, and the days around Eid (Lebaran) are the busiest travel days of the year, so boats and flights are packed. Respect fasting hours in public, book boats and flights early around Eid, and expect a quieter, more local feel.

  • Nyepi (Bali Day of Silence) 9 Mar, Mon 8 Mar to Wed 10 Mar

    Bali shuts down for 24 hours. The airport closes, nobody goes outside, and there is no traffic or noise. Nusa Lembongan observes it too. Be in your hostel the day before and don't book a flight or boat that day. It can be a very peaceful day. Confirm the date locally.

Know before you go

Indonesia

Money

  • Indonesia uses the rupiah (IDR), in notes of tens of thousands. A couple of million rupiah can be a few dozen dollars.
  • Use ATMs attached to banks, not stand-alone ones in shops, and cover the PIN pad. Card skimming happens.

Getting online

  • Tourist eSIMs work well in Bali and Lombok. Local SIMs (Telkomsel is the strongest) are sold at the airport.
  • Grab and Gojek work for rides and food in Bali. On the Gilis there are no apps, only bikes and horse carts.

Getting around

  • Bali traffic is slow. Allow double the time you expect, and use Grab or Gojek for fixed prices.
  • Fast boats from Bali to the Gilis and Lembongan are weather-dependent, can be rough, and sometimes leave late. Take sea-sickness tablets and don't cut a boat day close to a flight home.

Scooters + licences

  • Foreign riders must carry their home licence and an International Driving Permit with the motorcycle category. Police run regular checks in Bali hotspots, and fines are on the spot.
  • Bali's traffic and roads cause many tourist injuries every year, and your travel insurance may not pay out if you weren't correctly licensed.

Staying safe

  • Methanol poisoning from fake spirits is a real danger in Bali, Lombok and the Gilis. Stick to sealed bottles, beer, and venues you trust, and be wary of cheap cocktails and free shots.
  • Monkeys at Uluwatu Temple snatch sunglasses and phones. Hold your things tight.

Manners

  • Cover shoulders and knees at temples, wear a sarong where asked, and don't step on the offerings on the pavement.
  • On Lombok and the Gilis most people are Muslim. Dress modestly away from the beach, and be respectful during Ramadan.

Health

  • Don't drink tap water, even to brush your teeth, and avoid ice if you're unsure.
  • "Bali belly" is common. Take rehydration salts and go easy on street food in your first days.
