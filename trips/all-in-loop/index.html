Festivals and closures to plan around

  • Christmas + New Year peak Sun 20 Dec to Tue 5 Jan

    The busiest and dearest weeks of the year across the region. Popular hostels, island boats and flights sell out. Book early and avoid one-night stops. It is worth paying for a bed you have held.

  • Tet (Vietnamese New Year) 4–10 Feb, Mon 1 Feb to Thu 11 Feb

    Vietnam's biggest holiday. Many shops, restaurants and tour operators close for days, trains and buses sell out a week either side, and fares jump. Book every bus, train and flight early, expect some closures, and base yourself on the coast. Or travel outside it.

  • Vietnam National Day Wed 1 Sep to Sun 5 Sep

    A long weekend. Locals travel, so beach towns and Hanoi trains are busy. Book trains and coastal beds ahead.

Know before you go

Riding a scooter? Read this first.
  • Most travel insurers will only pay out if you hold the right licence for the bike you were riding. Read your policy wording, and ask them in writing.
  • Wear a proper helmet every ride, closed shoes and long trousers. Don't ride tired, drunk or in the dark.
  • Photograph the bike on all sides before you ride, and never hand over your passport as a deposit.
  • If you've never ridden, the road is not the place to learn. Take a lesson, or join a guided ride with a driver.

Vietnam

Money

  • Vietnam uses the dong (VND), and the numbers are big. Take care with the zeros on notes.
  • ATMs are everywhere in cities. Limits per withdrawal are low, so you pay a fee more often.

Getting online

  • A tourist eSIM is the easiest. Local SIMs (Viettel, Vinaphone) are cheap with a passport.
  • Grab works in Hanoi and Saigon for rides, food and parcels, so it is useful for much more than taxis.

Getting around

  • Use Grab for taxis and bikes. It is cheaper and removes the meter arguments.
  • Crossing the road: walk at a steady pace, don't stop or run, and the traffic flows round you.

Scooters + licences

  • Vietnam wants a valid motorcycle licence plus an International Driving Permit under the 1968 convention with the motorcycle category (A or A1). Many US, Canadian and Australian permits are issued under the older 1949 convention and may not count.
  • Riding without the right licence risks a fine and the bike being impounded, and it is likely to affect your insurance.

Staying safe

  • Vapes and e-cigarettes are banned in Vietnam, and tourists have been fined for vaping.
  • Bags and phones are snatched from motorbikes. Carry your bag away from the road, and don't hold your phone out near traffic.

Manners

  • Cover shoulders and knees at pagodas and temples.
  • Take your shoes off when you enter a home, and point your feet away from the family altar.

Health

  • Drink bottled or filtered water, and brush your teeth with it.
  • Air quality in Hanoi can be poor in winter. Check the AQI if you have asthma.
