Festivals and closures to plan around

  • Christmas + New Year peak Sun 20 Dec to Tue 5 Jan

    The busiest and dearest weeks of the year across the region. Popular hostels, island boats and flights sell out. Book early and avoid one-night stops. It is worth paying for a bed you have held.

  • Pi Mai (Lao New Year) Wed 14 Apr to Fri 16 Apr

    Water festival and processions. Luang Prabang is at its best and busiest, and many businesses close. Book Luang Prabang beds and the Kuang Si trip ahead.

The practical stuff

Laos

Money

  • Laos uses the kip (LAK), and the notes are huge. Bring clean US dollars or Thai baht as a backup.
  • ATMs are in the main towns, with low limits and fees. Take out what you need before heading to small places.
  • Many places take cash only. Cards work in some larger hotels and restaurants.
  • Haggling at markets is friendly and expected, but keep it light.

Getting online

  • Tourist eSIMs work in the main towns, and coverage thins out in the mountains.
  • Local SIMs (Lao Telecom, Unitel) are sold with a passport. Download maps offline.
  • Hostel wifi can be slow. Don't plan on streaming.

Getting around

  • The Laos-China railway makes Luang Prabang to Vang Vieng and Vientiane quick and easy. Book tickets ahead, and allow time for the station transfers.
  • The slow boat from Thailand takes two days with a night in Pakbeng. Take food, a cushion and a warm layer.
  • Tuk-tuks are priced per vehicle. Agree before you set off.

Scooters + licences

  • Foreigners are expected to carry a valid licence plus an International Driving Permit, and an accident without one can become very expensive.
  • Roads are narrow and often unlit, with animals on them. Avoid riding after dark.
  • Laos insurance for a rental is often thin. Ask exactly what is covered, and check your travel insurance too.

Staying safe

  • Vapes are banned in Laos. Leave yours at home.
  • Unexploded bombs from the war still lie in parts of the country. Stay on marked paths and never touch anything metal in the countryside.
  • In Vang Vieng, river levels change fast. Don't tube drunk, and skip it when the water is high.
  • Tap water is not safe. Watch your drink on nights out.

Manners

  • Dress modestly at temples, and never point your feet at a Buddha statue.
  • At the dawn alms giving in Luang Prabang, watch quietly from a respectful distance and don't buy from the sellers who push you. Don't use flash.
  • Don't touch a person's head, and use two hands (or touch your right elbow) when giving or receiving.

Health

  • Drink only bottled or filtered water.
  • Malaria risk exists in rural areas, and dengue is widespread. Ask a travel clinic about both.
  • Medical care is basic outside Vientiane. Make sure your insurance covers evacuation.

Questions people ask

Do I need a visa for Laos?

It depends on your passport. UK, US, Australian, Canadian and EU passports get 30 days on a visa on arrival or e-visa. Check the official site before you fly. Checked Oct 2026.

What is the best time to visit Laos?

Best months: Jan, Nov and Dec. Expect rain or rough seas in Mar and Apr.

How much does it cost to backpack Laos?

Dorm beds at Mad Monkey in Laos start from about $6 a night. With transport, activities and food, most travellers plan on roughly $40 to $80 a day depending on style.

Is Laos safe for solo travellers?

Millions of people backpack Laos solo. Stay in social hostels, keep your valuables close, use app-based taxis where they exist and read the safety notes below, especially if you plan to ride a scooter.
