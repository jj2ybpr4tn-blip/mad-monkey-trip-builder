Festivals and closures to plan around

  • Christmas + New Year peak Sun 20 Dec to Tue 5 Jan

    The busiest and dearest weeks of the year across the region. Popular hostels, island boats and flights sell out. Book early and avoid one-night stops. It is worth paying for a bed you have held.

  • Chinese New Year crowds Thu 4 Feb to Sun 14 Feb

    Big regional travel peak. Flights, hotels and popular day tours fill up and cost more, especially Bangkok, Phuket and Siem Reap. Book transport and your Angkor or island trips early.

  • Khmer New Year Tue 13 Apr to Fri 16 Apr

    Cambodians travel home. Phnom Penh goes quiet, buses to Siem Reap and the coast fill, and the water-throwing starts. Book buses and boats early. Siem Reap is lively, Phnom Penh is sleepy.

  • Pchum Ben Wed 29 Sep to Fri 1 Oct

    Cambodia's ancestors' festival. Many people return to their home towns, so buses fill up and some businesses close. Book buses early and don't be surprised by closed shops.

The practical stuff

Cambodia

Money

  • Cambodia runs on US dollars alongside the riel. ATMs dispense dollars, and small change comes back in riel (4,000 riel is about $1).
  • Bring crisp, undamaged notes. Torn or marked dollars are often refused.
  • There are few ATMs on the islands. Take cash with you to Koh Rong, Koh Sdach and Kampot's countryside.
  • Tuk-tuk drivers quote a price, and a polite bargain is normal.

Getting online

  • Local SIMs (Cellcard, Smart, Metfone) are cheap and sold at the airport with your passport. A tourist eSIM is easier.
  • Coverage is good in towns and patchy on the islands.
  • Download maps offline for island and temple days.

Getting around

  • PassApp and Grab work in Phnom Penh and Siem Reap. Tuk-tuks are great for temple days: agree a day rate with the driver.
  • Buses between Phnom Penh, Siem Reap, Kampot and the coast are cheap and frequent. Book the day before in busy periods.
  • The Thailand land border has been closed, so check the current status before planning a crossing.

Scooters + licences

  • Check the rules and the rental company's insurance before you ride. Riding without a valid licence can void your insurance and leave you liable.
  • Roads outside the cities are rough and shared with animals and unlit trucks. Avoid riding at night.
  • Never leave your passport as a deposit.

Staying safe

  • Vapes are banned in Cambodia. Leave yours at home.
  • Bag-snatching from passing motorbikes is common in Phnom Penh. Carry your bag away from the road, and keep your phone away from the kerb.
  • Don't buy drugs. Penalties are real.
  • Don't give money to children who beg, or visit orphanage "tours". Both feed the problem.

Manners

  • Cover your shoulders and knees at Angkor and at every pagoda. Temples can turn you away.
  • Don't touch monks (women), don't point your feet at Buddha images, and don't touch heads.
  • S21 and the Killing Fields are sites of genocide. Be quiet and respectful, and don't pose for photos.

Health

  • Drink bottled or filtered water.
  • Dengue is common. Use repellent and wear light long sleeves at dusk.
  • Rabies is a risk from stray dogs and monkeys. If bitten, get medical help immediately.
  • Hospitals in Phnom Penh and Siem Reap are fine, and the islands have only clinics. Check your insurance covers a transfer.

Questions people ask

Do I need a visa for Cambodia?

It depends on your passport. UK, US, Australian, Canadian and EU passports get 30 days on a visa on arrival or e-visa. Check the official site before you fly. Checked Oct 2026.

What is the best time to visit Cambodia?

Best months: Jan, Feb, Mar, Nov and Dec. Expect rain or rough seas in Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep and Oct.

How much does it cost to backpack Cambodia?

Dorm beds at Mad Monkey in Cambodia start from about $5 a night. With transport, activities and food, most travellers plan on roughly $40 to $80 a day depending on style.

Is Cambodia safe for solo travellers?

Millions of people backpack Cambodia solo. Stay in social hostels, keep your valuables close, use app-based taxis where they exist and read the safety notes below, especially if you plan to ride a scooter.
