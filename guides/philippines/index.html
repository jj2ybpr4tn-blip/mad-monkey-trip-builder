Festivals and closures to plan around

  • Christmas + New Year peak Sun 20 Dec to Tue 5 Jan

    The busiest and dearest weeks of the year across the region. Popular hostels, island boats and flights sell out. Book early and avoid one-night stops. It is worth paying for a bed you have held.

  • Holy Week (Semana Santa) Sun 21 Mar to Mon 29 Mar

    The Philippines nearly stops from Maundy Thursday to Easter. Transport is jammed, many shops and bars shut, and some places don't sell alcohol. Siquijor has its famous Holy Week healing festival. Book ferries and flights early and have a plan for Thursday and Friday.

The practical stuff

Philippines

Money

  • The Philippines uses pesos (PHP). ATMs are plentiful in cities and sparse on islands, so bring cash for Siquijor, Siargao and El Nido.
  • Many island tours charge a small environmental fee at the dock. Keep small notes.
  • GCash is popular with locals, but most visitors will rely on cash and cards.

Getting online

  • A tourist eSIM is the easiest. Local SIMs (Globe, Smart) are sold at the airport.
  • Island signal is patchy and boat rides have none. Download maps and boarding passes offline.
  • Register on the eTravel site before you fly, and keep the QR code.

Getting around

  • Between islands you use ferries and short flights. Weather can cancel both, so leave a spare day before an international flight home.
  • In cities, Grab works in Manila and Cebu. Jeepneys are the cheap, crowded local option. Ask the driver or the person next to you where to get off.
  • Tricycles are priced per ride. Agree before you set off.

Scooters + licences

  • Scooters are the way to see Siquijor and Siargao, but check the rental company's insurance and keep your licence with you. Local conditions mean many tourists end up in the clinic.
  • Wear a helmet, and avoid riding at night or after rain on loose roads.
  • Never hand over your passport as a deposit.

Staying safe

  • Typhoon season runs roughly June to November. Check the forecast and have a flexible plan.
  • Keep a copy of your passport, and don't flash expensive gear in crowded areas in Manila.
  • Don't buy drugs. The law and its enforcement are harsh.
  • Choose island-hopping boats with life jackets, and say no to overloaded ones.

Manners

  • Filipinos are warm and talkative. A smile and "salamat" (thank you) go a long way.
  • Dress modestly at churches, and keep quiet during Mass.
  • Holy Week (Semana Santa) is a deeply felt, serious time, and many places close. Plan around it.

Health

  • Drink bottled or filtered water.
  • Dengue spikes in the rainy season. Use repellent, especially at dawn and dusk.
  • Island clinics are basic. Make sure your insurance covers evacuation to a bigger hospital.
  • Heat and humidity are strong. Drink more than you think you need.

Questions people ask

Do I need a visa for Philippines?

It depends on your passport. UK, US, Australian, Canadian and EU passports get 30 days visa-free. Check the official site before you fly. Checked Oct 2026.

What is the best time to visit Philippines?

Best months: Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May and Dec. Expect rain or rough seas in Jul, Aug and Sep.

How much does it cost to backpack Philippines?

Dorm beds at Mad Monkey in Philippines start from about $8 a night. With transport, activities and food, most travellers plan on roughly $40 to $80 a day depending on style.

Is Philippines safe for solo travellers?

Millions of people backpack Philippines solo. Stay in social hostels, keep your valuables close, use app-based taxis where they exist and read the safety notes below, especially if you plan to ride a scooter.
